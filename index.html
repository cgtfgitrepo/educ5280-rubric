Thinking (Analyze): questioning and investigating The follow-up question cannot be tested against the materials; evidence is missing or irrelevant. Poses a testable question but selects general or loosely relevant evidence. Poses a testable question and uses it to select relevant evidence from the novel and the source. Thinking (Evaluate): reflecting on the interpretation Gives an opinion without evidence and does not revisit the first reading. States whether the reading changed, with little reasoning. Explains whether the evidence confirmed or changed the first reading and poses a relevant next question. Justifies keeping or revising the reading with precise reasons and poses a next question that extends the inquiry.

States a claim, but evidence and explanation are loosely organised; some terms and conventions are inaccurate. Adapted from Inquiry-Based Learning Rubric, by Algoma District School Board, 2026.

Note

Categories follow the inquiry process of questioning, investigating, reflecting and discussing (Uselman, 2019); the Bloom levels in brackets show the thinking each category draws on and organise the teacher’s questions.

Scoring Criteria

Score each category from 1 to 4 (total /24). The overall level is the average score rounded to the nearest level: 6–8 = Level 1, 9–14 = Level 2, 15–20 = Level 3, 21–24 = Level 4. Questioning and investigating is the core of the inquiry, so a score of 1 there limits the overall level to 2.
